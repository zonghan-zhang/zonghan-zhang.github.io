Short Biography. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Mississippi State University, advised by Prof. Zhiqian Chen. My research lies at the intersection of machine learning, network science, and uncertainty quantification. I develop uncertainty-aware and interpretable machine learning frameworks for modeling, understanding, and intervening in complex networked systems.

A central theme of my work is to move beyond purely data-driven prediction by integrating structured inductive biases, probabilistic reasoning, and mechanistic insights into graph-based AI models. My dissertation, Toward a Unified Network Flow Framework: From Conservation Principles to Fluid Dynamics Models, studies how information, risk, influence, and resources propagate through interconnected systems, and how reliable AI methods can support decision-making under uncertainty.

Before joining Mississippi State University, I received an M.S. in Information Science from the University of Pittsburgh and a master's degree in Human Resource Management from Rutgers University.

Research Interests

My research lies at the intersection of machine learning, network science, and uncertainty quantification. I develop uncertainty-aware and interpretable models for complex networked systems, with a focus on structured inductive bias, diffusion dynamics, and decision-making under intervention.

My current research centers on physically informed network flow models, probabilistic graph learning, Bayesian optimization, sensitivity analysis, tensor representations for interconnected networks, and AI systems for socio-technical decision-making.

Teaching & Mentorship

My teaching philosophy emphasizes helping students understand not only how to build machine learning models, but how to reason about their reliability, limitations, and broader impact. I focus on developing structured thinking, uncertainty awareness, and interpretability alongside technical skills.
