Short Biography. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Mississippi State University, advised by Prof. Zhiqian Chen. My research lies at the intersection of machine learning, network science, and uncertainty quantification. I develop uncertainty-aware and interpretable machine learning frameworks for modeling, understanding, and intervening in complex networked systems.

A central theme of my work is to move beyond purely data-driven prediction by integrating structured inductive biases, probabilistic reasoning, and mechanistic insights into graph-based AI models. My dissertation, Toward a Unified Network Flow Framework: From Conservation Principles to Fluid Dynamics Models, studies how information, risk, influence, and resources propagate through interconnected systems, and how reliable AI methods can support decision-making under uncertainty.

Before joining Mississippi State University, I received an M.S. in Information Science from the University of Pittsburgh and a master's degree in Human Resource Management from Rutgers University.

Research

My research develops uncertainty-aware and interpretable machine learning methods for complex networked and socio-technical systems. I am particularly interested in graph-based modeling, probabilistic inference, sensitivity analysis, structured representation learning, and intervention design under uncertainty.

Uncertainty-aware machine learning for networked systems

I study how uncertainty propagates through graph-based processes and how probabilistic models can support reliable inference and decision-making from limited, noisy, or observational data.

Interpretable structural representations

I design decomposition-based and tensorized representations that expose higher-order interactions, directional dependencies, and cross-layer effects in complex networked systems.

Mechanistic and physically informed graph learning

I develop graph-based models that incorporate physical and mechanistic principles, such as conservation, forcing effects, attenuation, and diffusion, to improve interpretability and generalization.

Intervention and decision-making in complex systems

I develop efficient algorithms for influence maximization, influence blocking, source localization, and policy-relevant intervention problems, with an emphasis on sample efficiency, interpretability, and uncertainty-aware reasoning.

Teaching and Mentoring

My teaching emphasizes both technical skill and critical reasoning. I aim to help students understand not only how to build machine learning and graph-based models, but also how to evaluate their assumptions, limitations, uncertainty, and broader impact.

I am prepared to teach undergraduate and graduate courses in data structures and algorithms, machine learning, artificial intelligence, graph machine learning, network science, modeling and simulation, and data mining. I am also interested in developing advanced courses on interpretable and trustworthy machine learning, uncertainty and decision-making in AI systems, and structured learning for networked and multilayer systems.
